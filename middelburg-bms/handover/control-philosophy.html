1Introduction

1.1Purpose

The warehouse stores pharmaceutical and surgical products, which must be kept within defined temperature and relative humidity limits, with records that demonstrate this to the regulator. This document defines:

  • how the BMS controls the warehouse HVAC plant to maintain those conditions;
  • how temperature and humidity are measured, recorded, evaluated and alarmed in every working area;
  • the alarm protocol: which alarms exist, who receives them and how they respond;
  • the screens and reports used to demonstrate storage conditions, including the annual trend report;
  • the preventive and scheduled maintenance protocol supported by the BMS;
  • the interface with the fire alarm system, remote access, data integrity and future integrations.

1.2Scope

Included: 6 hybrid (VRF) units serving the 6 warehouse supply air lines, 5 rooftop units serving the support areas, their 28 temperature and relative humidity measuring points, the 6 hybrid inlet fire dampers, the fire alarm interface, the Niagara JACE 9000 and the Spartcon BMS reporting platform.

4Storage condition requirements

4.1Regulatory basis

  • Temperature: controlled room temperature of 15–25 °C (WHO Good Storage and Distribution Practices). Storage areas below 25 °C (South African Pharmacy Council).
  • Relative humidity: dry storage at no more than 60 %RH (WHO).
  • Products with special storage conditions on their labels (for example 2–8 °C) must be stored in dedicated cold storage. Cold storage is outside the scope of this BMS.

4.2Limits configured in the BMS

ParameterNormal operationWarning (storage limit exceeded)Critical (severe excursion)
Space temperature15–25 °C, controlled around the seasonal setpointBelow 15 °C or above 25 °CBelow 13 °C or above 27 °C
Relative humidityUp to 60 %RHAbove 60 %RHAbove 65 %RH

The limits apply to every measuring point in the warehouse halls and support areas. Changes to limits are made only by the BMS administrator, with the written approval of the client's Quality Assurance, and are recorded.

4.3Seasonal temperature setpoint

The operating setpoint is set by the client, normally between 20 °C and 22 °C, which keeps the space well inside the 15–25 °C limits. The setpoint is adjusted seasonally: lower in the hot summer months, to keep the warmest parts of the halls below 25 °C, and higher in winter, to avoid unnecessary heating and to keep the coolest parts above 15 °C. The setpoint in use is recorded in the BMS history.

5HVAC control philosophy

5.1General principle

The BMS reads the temperature and relative humidity from all 28 measuring points and from every unit. Each unit regulates its own area to the setpoint, under the supervision of the JACE 9000. The reporting platform gives operators one view of all areas, records everything, and raises alarms when a limit is exceeded.

5.2Temperature control

  1. The space temperature of each line and support area is measured continuously.
  2. Each unit compares its measured temperature with the setpoint and drives its cooling or heating to hold the setpoint. Together, the six hybrid units keep a uniform temperature along all six warehouse lines.
  3. Rooftop units change over between cooling and heating automatically. They cool when the space is above the setpoint and heat when it falls below (setpoint − 1 °C). Between the two is a 1 °C dead band where they neither heat nor cool, which prevents rapid switching.
  4. If any measuring point leaves 15–25 °C, a temperature alarm is raised (section 7) and the excursion is recorded.

5.3Relative humidity control (60 %RH)

Humidity in the warehouse is brought in mainly by outdoor air. The hybrid (VRF) units control it by managing the ratio of outdoor air to return air, and by removing moisture on their cooling coils:

  1. Measure: relative humidity and temperature are read at the three points on each line.
  2. Recirculate: as humidity approaches the 60 %RH limit, the unit reduces outdoor air intake and increases the return (recirculated) air. Recirculated air passes the cooling coil repeatedly, so moisture keeps being removed and the RH drops without drawing in more humid outdoor air.
  3. Condense: the unit uses its cooling coil to condense moisture from the air, while temperature control holds the space at its setpoint.
  4. Ventilate when outdoor air is dry: when the outdoor relative humidity is lower than indoors, the unit can take up to about 30% outdoor air for normal ventilation without affecting humidity control or unit performance.
  5. Alarm: if any point exceeds 60 %RH, a humidity alarm is raised and the excursion is recorded until it returns below 60 %RH.

The outdoor/return air ratio and coil operation run automatically in each unit's CAREL controller. The JACE 9000 provides supervisory control, and the reporting platform monitors and records the result. Because the six units work in parallel, the building has redundancy: if one unit is out of service, the remaining units continue conditioning their lines while the fault is attended to.

6Fire safety interface

6.1Current arrangement: central shutdown

The fire alarm system provides a fire signal to the approved fire interlock. On a fire signal, the HVAC system is shut down centrally: the units stop, so that no air is blown into a fire, and the hybrid inlet fire dampers go to their fire (closed) position. Normal BMS commands are overridden by the fire interlock.

The fire shutdown must not depend solely on the JACE or on network communication. It is implemented through the approved independent (hard-wired or certified) fire interlock, in accordance with the fire design and the responsible fire engineer. A BMS operator cannot bypass or reset a fire interlock from the dashboard.

6.2Fire damper sequences

SequenceSteps
Normal startStart requested → inlet damper commanded open → damper-open proof confirmed (where fitted) → unit permitted to run.
Normal stopStop requested → unit stops → inlet damper commanded closed → damper-closed proof monitored.
FireFire signal → units stop → dampers to fire position → fire condition and damper states shown on the BMS → event recorded.
Reset and restorationFire condition investigated and cleared by authorised personnel → fire panel reset → interlock released → damper and unit checks → units returned to service with authorisation.

A difference between a damper command and its position feedback raises a damper fault alarm. A command alone is not taken as proof that a damper has moved.

6.3Monthly fire shutdown test

Every month, together with the responsible fire personnel, a test fire signal is generated to confirm that the system is fire compliant:

  1. All HVAC units shut down, and every inlet fire damper closes. Each is confirmed on the BMS.
  2. On reset, the dampers re-open and the units restart and return to normal operation.
  3. The result is recorded and certified in the fire compliance log, and the BMS event history is attached.

6.4Future: zone-based fire integration

With a future integration of an addressable fire alarm system, the BMS will be able to pinpoint the location of a fire and shut down only the units and dampers serving the affected area, keeping the rest of the warehouse conditioned. Until then, the fire signal shuts down the system centrally (section 6.1).

7.3Alarm handling

  • The dashboard shows an alarm counter on every screen. Each unit card shows each alarm point as OK or ALARM, and the Alarm Register lists all alarms (section 9.5).
  • The responsible person acknowledges the alarm, investigates, and records the cause and action taken. Acknowledging an alarm does not clear the underlying fault.
  • For storage-condition excursions, Quality Assurance assesses product impact using the excursion report (section 9.3) and the product stability data.
  • An alarm delay of up to 15 minutes may be applied to storage-condition warnings to avoid alarms from short events such as door openings. Critical alarms are not delayed.

8Maintenance protocol

The BMS supports three kinds of maintenance: emergency response to failures, preventive maintenance driven by measured equipment condition, and scheduled maintenance at fixed intervals.

8.1Emergency maintenance: system failure alarms

When a unit trips, or a sensor stops reading, the BMS raises a critical alarm that states which unit or sensor failed and what happened (for example "HYB-03 supply fan trip" or "Line 2 middle sensor not responding"). The alarm goes to the maintenance team (section 7.2). The other units continue operating, and the event, its duration and the conditions during it remain in the history.

8.2Preventive maintenance: filter condition from airflow and pressure

For every unit, the BMS monitors the supply airflow and the static air pressure against their design setpoints (design airflow: 8,000 L/s per hybrid unit). As filters load with dust, the pressure across them rises and the unit's airflow and pressure move away from the design values. Alarm rules:

  • Dirty filter alarm from the unit controller's filter switch.
  • Filter condition alarm when the static pressure deviates from its setpoint, or the airflow falls below its design value, by more than an agreed margin (initially 15%) for longer than 30 minutes.

The alarm tells the maintenance team which unit's filters need cleaning or replacement, before airflow loss affects temperature or humidity control.

8.3Preventive maintenance: refrigerant performance and re-gassing indicator

A unit that is losing refrigerant cools less effectively, so it has to move more air for longer to reach the same supply temperature. The BMS trends each unit's supply air temperature drop against its airflow over time:

  • A baseline ratio is recorded for each unit after commissioning and after each service (for example, the airflow and temperature drop at a known load).
  • If, over the trend period, the unit needs about 20% more airflow for the same temperature drop (or achieves a 20% lower temperature drop at the same airflow), a refrigerant performance alarm is raised. It indicates that a gas pressure check and possible re-gassing are needed.
  • Where the VRF controllers make refrigerant pressures available over Modbus, these are read and trended as well, and an alarm is raised on low pressure.

8.4Scheduled maintenance plan

The BMS raises a reminder when each task is due for each unit. Intervals follow the manufacturers' recommendations and the maintenance philosophy per unit, and may be shortened based on the condition alarms in 8.2 and 8.3.

Each maintenance task and alarm response is recorded with date, unit, task, findings and technician, and closed on the BMS. Maintenance records, together with the BMS history, form part of the evidence that the storage facility is maintained in a state of control (WHO TRS 961 Annex 9, Supplement 5).

11Remote access and mobile access

11.1Secure remote access

Authorised users can open the BMS from anywhere through an encrypted private network (Tailscale) between their device and the BMS computer on site. No ports are opened to the internet, and the JACE is never exposed directly to the public internet. Access is granted per person by invitation, and is removed when a person leaves. Step-by-step instructions are in the Remote Access Setup Guide.

11.2Mobile access

The BMS dashboard works in the web browser of any phone, tablet or computer (Android, iOS, Windows, macOS), adapting its layout to the screen. Supervisors and maintenance staff can therefore check conditions, alarms and trends from their phones today.

11.3Future: Android and iOS apps

As a future integration, the BMS will be available as an installable app for Android and iOS, giving easy access to monitoring and the permitted controls, with push notifications for alarms.

11.4Why this is efficient

  • Faster response: the right person is alerted on their phone the moment a limit or a unit fails, instead of when someone next looks at a screen. Excursions are shorter and product risk is lower.
  • Fewer site visits: engineers can diagnose a fault remotely (which unit, which alarm, what the trend shows) and arrive with the right parts.
  • Evidence at hand: managers and Quality Assurance can review conditions and reports at any time, without being on site.
  • Preventive rather than reactive: condition alarms (filters, refrigerant) and maintenance reminders reach the maintenance team before problems affect storage conditions.

13Temperature and humidity mapping, and calibration

13.1Mapping study

A temperature and humidity mapping study measures conditions at many points across the storage areas (floor to the top of the racking, near doors, under the roof and near the supply grilles), using calibrated data loggers over a defined period. It identifies hot, cold and humid spots. Mapping is performed before use, in the hottest and coldest seasons, and after significant changes to the HVAC system, layout or racking (WHO TRS 961 Annex 9, Supplement 8). It is normally carried out by a qualified validation provider, under a protocol approved by Quality Assurance.

13.2How the BMS supports mapping

  • During a study, the BMS records all 28 measuring points and the operating state of every unit every 5 minutes, for comparison with the data loggers.
  • The mapping results confirm, or adjust, the positions of the measuring points so that they represent the worst-case locations in each area.
  • The annual trend report shows seasonal behaviour, which helps decide when re-mapping is needed.

13.3Sensor calibration

  • Temperature and RH sensors used as storage records are calibrated annually against traceable reference instruments, and the certificates are kept on file.
  • The BMS shows each sensor's last and next calibration date and flags overdue calibration.
  • Acceptance tolerances are set by Quality Assurance (typically ±0.5 °C and ±3 %RH).

Phase 1: in service (handed over 11 Nov 2026)Live monitoring of all units, temperature, humidity and fire damper status; RTU start/stop and setpoint control, with read-back verification; alarms on the dashboard; trends, period summary, MKT, excursion report and HVAC history; recording of every point every 5 minutes, with data export; secure remote access; operator training and user guide.
Phase 2: alarm protocol and maintenanceAlarm notifications to the responsible persons by SMS, WhatsApp and e-mail, with escalation and acknowledgement records (section 7.2); filter condition and refrigerant performance indicators (sections 8.2–8.3); scheduled maintenance reminders and records (section 8.4); annual trend report generated directly from the dashboard (section 10.2); individual user logins and a central audit trail.
Phase 3: future integrationsAndroid and iOS apps with push notifications (section 11.3); zone-based fire integration, to pinpoint the fire location and shut down only the affected units (section 6.4); refrigerant pressure monitoring from the VRF controllers; energy monitoring and optimisation.

The system architecture (section 3) already provides for these functions: all units and controllers are integrated on the JACE 9000, and the reporting platform records every point, so each phase adds functions without changes to the installed plant.
