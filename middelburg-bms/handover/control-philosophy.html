1Introduction

1.1Purpose

This document defines how the BMS controls the warehouse HVAC plant and how it monitors, alarms and reports warehouse temperature and relative humidity. It describes each screen and report used to show that pharmaceutical products are stored within approved conditions. It is intended for the client's management and Quality Assurance, and as a reference for inspections.

1.2Scope

  • HVAC control philosophy: 5 rooftop units and 11 hybrid units, plus fire damper status.
  • Environmental monitoring: temperature and relative humidity measurement, logging, limits and evaluation.
  • Alarm philosophy, and the recommended response.
  • Screen-by-screen details of what is displayed and reported, and the proposed reporting schedule.
  • Data integrity, retention, temperature/humidity mapping and calibration.

Out of scope: the design and installation of the HVAC plant, the JACE station programming (by the controls integrator), and the mapping study itself (section 9).

3HVAC control philosophy

3.1General principle

Each HVAC unit regulates temperature with its own controller and the JACE station logic. The BMS dashboard is supervisory: it gives visibility of all plant and conditions, records them, raises alarms, and lets authorised operators start/stop the rooftop units and change their setpoints. The BMS does not replace the units' own safety and regulation functions.

3.2Rooftop units (RTU-01…05)

FunctionPhilosophy
Start / stopCommanded by an operator from the BMS (point Uni{n}StartCmd). The supply fan status (Unit{n}SAF) confirms that the unit runs. A command the unit does not follow is shown as "Command accepted, unit not following".
Temperature setpointSet by an operator from the BMS (Unit{n}Setpoint), 15–35 °C in 0.5 °C steps. The setpoint must be chosen to keep the zone inside the approved acceptable range. Proposed normal setpoint: [TO CONFIRM, e.g. 20–22 °C].
Heating / coolingAutomatic changeover within the unit. It cools when the space temperature is above the setpoint and heats when it is below (setpoint − 1 °C). Between the two is a 1 °C dead band where the unit neither heats nor cools, which prevents rapid switching. The mode (Unit{n}Mode, true = heating) is displayed only and not commanded from the BMS.
HumidityRelative humidity is monitored only; there is no humidity control (humidifier/dehumidifier) point on this site. High humidity is managed through HVAC operation and building measures. [TO CONFIRM whether dehumidification is required]
Unit alarmUnit{n}Alarm is displayed as a unit trip / alarm (true = alarm, [TO CONFIRM]).

3.3Hybrid units (HYB-01…11)

Hybrid units operate under their own controllers. The BMS monitors only: BMS enable, mode (cool/heat/fan only), supply fan, fan speed, airflow, static pressure and setpoint, room temperature average, and the alarms for supply fan trip, no airflow, static pressure failure, room sensor 1/2 failure, on-coil sensor failure and dirty filter. No hybrid point can be written from the BMS (enforced on the host PC).

3.4Fire dampers

The six fire dampers on hybrids 6–11 are shown as OPEN or CLOSED, read-only (true = OPEN, [TO CONFIRM]). Fire damper operation is the responsibility of the fire system. The BMS does not command dampers.

3.5Operator commands

  • Only users with the Operator role can send commands; Viewers can only monitor.
  • Every command is confirmed in a dialog (old value → new value, optional reason) and recorded in the Audit Trail.
  • Commands are written at priority 1 and verified by reading the value back from the controller. The result is shown as Confirmed, or as a clear failure.
  • Only 10 points can be written (rooftop start/stop and setpoint). This is enforced on the host PC, whatever a browser sends.

4.2Measurement, logging and data quality

Display updateAll points read every 15 seconds.
RecordingEvery point of every unit and sensor is recorded every 5 minutes, aligned to the clock, in files on the host PC (one file per day, plus one per point per month).
Time baseSouth African Standard Time (SAST, UTC+2), from the host PC clock. The host PC clock must be synchronised to an internet or network time source [TO CONFIRM].
Data quality statesLIVE = current. STALE = no new value within twice the read interval. OFFLINE = no response. NOT MAPPED = point does not exist. OUT OF RANGE = implausible value. Only LIVE values are displayed as readings and used in evaluations.
GapsFailed reads are recorded with the reason, so gaps are explicit. Trend lines break at gaps, and a gap ends an excursion.

4.5Calculated values

Min / max / meanOver the selected period, per zone and parameter, from recorded values only.
Outside rangePercentage of recorded samples beyond the alert limits.
Samples with dataNumber of samples received out of the number expected, which shows data completeness (for example 288 / 288 for 24 h at 5-minute intervals).
Mean kinetic temperature (MKT)MKT = (ΔH/R) / −ln( (1/n) Σ e−ΔH/(R·Ti) ), with Ti in kelvin, ΔH = 83.144 kJ/mol and R = 8.3144 J/(mol·K), so ΔH/R = 10 000 K. This is the conventional activation energy used in WHO guidance. It is calculated per zone from the recorded temperatures (on the Conditions screen over the last 30 days from hourly values; on Trends over the selected period).
ExcursionA continuous run of recorded values outside the alert limits, reported with start, end, duration, direction, peak value and severity. A data gap ends an excursion.
Note: MKT and excursion figures are calculations on the recorded data. Whether a product is affected by an excursion is a quality decision, made using the product's stability data.

5.2Alarm presentation

  • An alarm counter on the bell in the top bar and next to "Alarms" in the menu, on every screen.
  • On the unit card, each alarm point is shown as green OK or red ALARM. The filter shows "Clean" or "DIRTY – replace filter".
  • The Alarm Register (section 6.6) lists all alarms, with severity, time raised, source, description, current reading, threshold and status.
  • Zone cards on the Conditions screen and the 3D model are coloured by the worst state.

9Temperature and humidity mapping, and calibration

9.1Mapping versus monitoring

Mapping studyContinuous monitoring (this BMS)
PurposeFind how temperature and humidity vary across the storage area, including hot, cold and humid spots, and where to place permanent sensorsShow, record and alarm conditions 24/7 at fixed points
HowMany calibrated data loggers in a 3-D grid (floor to high racking, near doors, roof and HVAC) for a defined periodFixed sensors, read every 15 s and recorded every 5 min
WhenBefore use, in the hottest and coldest seasons, and after significant changes (HVAC, layout, racking)Continuously
WhoUsually a qualified validation service provider, with a protocol and report approved by QASpartcon (system), with site staff and QA (review)

9.2How the BMS supports mapping

  • During the study, the BMS records HVAC operating states and its own sensor values every 5 minutes, for comparison with the loggers.
  • After the study, the official monitoring sensors (the room sensors, plus extra sensors if needed) are positioned at the worst-case locations it identifies. The zones and limits in the BMS are then updated.
  • Stored logs provide the data for seasonal reviews and for deciding when re-mapping is needed.

Mapping status: [TO CONFIRM: has a mapping study been done for this warehouse? If so, please provide the report so that sensor positions can be aligned. If not, Spartcon recommends commissioning one (summer and winter).]

9.3Sensor calibration

  • Sensors used as official storage records should be calibrated against traceable references at a defined interval [TO CONFIRM, typically annually], with certificates kept on file.
  • The Conditions screen shows the last and next calibration date per zone sensor and flags overdue calibration. The dates must be entered from the certificates.
  • The tolerance for acceptance (for example ±0.5 °C, ±3 %RH) is to be set by QA [TO CONFIRM].
